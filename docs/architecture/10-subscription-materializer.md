# 10: Subscription Materializer

The **subscription materializer** hosts `subql` and turns its per-consumer output into per-session wire output for each client. It is the only server piece that knows about client sessions and the reliability policy. Authorization is split at the boundary. The visibility trait is defined in `subql` and the materializer holds the implementation. This chapter pins the `subql`/connetto boundary as it stands and fixes where reliability and retry sit. The mutation and CDC paths are [03-sync-pipeline.md](03-sync-pipeline.md), subscription registration and live delivery are [04-subscriptions.md](04-subscriptions.md), the aggregate path is [05-aggregate-queries.md](05-aggregate-queries.md), the resume cursor and oplog catchup are [06-reconnect.md](06-reconnect.md), the OpenFGA call the auth filter depends on is [08-authorization.md](08-authorization.md), fan-out is [17-fan-out.md](17-fan-out.md), and the in-process side of the boundary is [subql.md](subql.md).

## What subql owns (the materializer only drives it)

`subql` is an in-process library with no async runtime of its own. The materializer supplies the runtime, the connections, and the visibility and upkeep implementations, then drives `subql`. The library owns:

- **CDC ingestion.** The `CdcSource` trait plus the concrete sources. `PgStreamingCdcSource` holds a `pg_walstream` replication connection, issues `START_REPLICATION`, stamps each event with its LSN, and flows acks back so Postgres can recycle WAL. `PollingPgCdcSource` drains a slot over plain SQL, and `SqliteCdcSource` is the client-side source. The WAL parsers for pgoutput, wal2json v1 and v2, and Maxwell feed one path. The materializer drives the consume loop (`next_item` and `ack`) and decides the ack cadence and the reconnect policy, but it does not parse WAL or hold the replication protocol.
- **Matching and classification.** The subscription registry, routing by referenced tables, the bitmap-indexed candidate prune, the predicate VM with SQL three-valued logic, and UPDATE transition detection. A base UPDATE surfaces per consumer as `inserted`, `deleted`, or `updated` when a row enters, leaves, or changes within its result set.
- **Aggregate IVM.** The delta family and its `GROUP BY` variants, maintained in memory and emitted as signed deltas. [05-aggregate-queries.md](05-aggregate-queries.md) owns the family, the tiers, and which queries fall out to re-execution.
- **Event to patchset conversion.** The emit builders fold a batch of events over a source-agnostic catalog into one `sqlite-diff-rs` patchset or changeset.
- **Inbound apply.** `apply_diffset_bytes` (and its async variant) parses uploaded SQLite session bytes, dispatches the patchset or changeset marker, reconstructs against the catalog, and applies transactionally through the diesel adapters for Postgres, MySQL, and SQLite. No SQL casts.
- **Re-execution engine.** `AutoResolvingEngine` in async mode. It classifies the queries the row-image engine cannot resolve, owns trigger servicing, per-subscription debounce, key batching, and paging, and drives the caller's connector for every database read. It owns the read queue and the coalescing.
- **Predicate-state persistence and position types.** Durable shards for predicate state, and the `Checkpoint` family.
- **Visibility and upkeep primitives.** The `VisibilityPolicy` trait plus the ready-made `RowPolicy`, `OpenFgaPolicy`, and `Shapes` implementations, and the FGA upkeep primitives. `Shapes::diff` computes what a change moved from its shape, `OpenFgaPolicy::apply` writes that diff to the store, and the replay mechanism re-derives the queries a changed row feeds. A row becoming invisible is delivered as a delete, and the check runs against both versions of the row with the previous-version check made only when the current version is absent or invisible, through `subql::visibility::transition::transitions` on both the live and the catchup path.

The line to hold onto. `subql` runs re-execution queries through a connector the materializer supplies the setup for, and it holds no retry anywhere. Everything that can transiently fail crosses a connection the materializer owns or configures.

## What the materializer owns

- **Sessions and the wire.** `subql` speaks in consumer ids, typed events, deltas, and patchset bytes. It has no notion of a WebSocket session, the flow-control window, keepalive, the reconnect handshake, or the `connetto-core` `ControlMessage` and `BulkMessage`. The materializer maps `subql`'s per-consumer output onto sessions and frames it into `SnapshotPatch`, `LivePatch`, and aggregate envelopes carrying the resume `Cursor`, back-pressured per session.
- **The fan-out unit.** One change event, not one subscriber. Everything a change costs is charged once per event, and the only work charged per subscriber is the socket write. [17-fan-out.md](17-fan-out.md) owns the shape, what stays proportional to subscriber count and why that is acceptable, and the protocol and materializer changes required to adopt it.
- **Authorization.** All of connetto's visibility call sites ask through `subql::visibility::VisibilityPolicy`, with the row-level-secured policy behind it. The trait is defined low and consumed upward because its callers sit on both sides of the boundary. The change path is the one that moves into `subql`, while the catchup, write, and capability-minting paths stay in connetto. Write authority is gated too. Every inbound mutation is checked before apply, and the gate is in [08-authorization.md](08-authorization.md).
- **The FGA upkeep driver.** `subql` ships the primitives, and connetto's `FgaUpkeep` (the [`connetto_server::openfga::StoreUpkeep`] implementation in [openfga.rs](../../crates/connetto-server/src/openfga.rs)) drives them per changed row on the dispatch path. It runs `Shapes::diff` on the event, applies the diff through `OpenFgaPolicy::apply` before the event is dispatched, re-runs the replayed queries as the deployment rather than as a caller, and computes the grant moves the session layer resyncs on. It runs before delivery, because until the replayed queries have run the store still states what the change took away. connetto also owns the startup refusal for a policy shape whose facts it cannot unwind, [`connetto_server::openfga::SetupError::Unwithdrawable`], which leaves no booting deployment able to produce a shape that would hand a grant to somebody whose access has already gone.
- **Per-session patchset assembly.** `subql`'s builder folds a slice of events into one patchset. Because per-subscription filtering differs per client, the materializer selects each session's matched event subset and invokes the builder for it. The visibility answer for each row comes from the trait `subql` defines, not from a check the materializer performs independently. The primitive is `subql`'s and the per-session selection is the materializer's.
- **The write path end to end.** A client uploads a `MutationPatch`. The materializer authorizes the write, detects conflicts on the `updated_at` token, calls `subql`'s `apply_diffset_bytes` in a transaction against its Postgres pool, and reports the outcome. The CDC echo serves as the success ack, so only `MutationReject` and `MutationConflict` are dedicated messages. `client_seq` idempotency and the reply are the materializer's.
- **Oplog, reconnect, and catchup.** The retention-bounded oplog keyed by commit position, the catchup versus `FullResyncRequired` decision, and tombstones are session-bound server state. See [06-reconnect.md](06-reconnect.md). The catchup path carries the same two-version authorization obligation as the live path and meets it from the stored change event, which carries both row images. Each oplog entry also carries the compressed patch that [`connetto_server::materializer::Materializer::dispatch`] folded once when it logged the event, matched or not, so catchup sends those bytes and builds nothing for a record its caller may not see.
- **Reliability orchestration.** `subql` keeps zero retry surface on purpose. The materializer is where one backoff primitive covers CDC reconnect, re-execution retry, FGA retry, delivery back-pressure, and mutation retry.

This boundary is normative. It is the contract that lets the reliability and retry policy live in exactly one place.

## Reliability and retry

The retry policy lives here because everything that can transiently fail in the subscription pipeline crosses the materializer's boundary with Postgres, OpenFGA, or the network. `subql`'s event processing (matching, aggregate maintenance, conversion) is deterministic in-memory work and holds no retry surface. The connections it needs (the `CdcSource` and the re-exec connector) are supplied and driven by the materializer, so their failures are retried here too.

### Failure inventory

| # | Failure | Owner | Idempotency key | Policy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CDC source drop (replication slot or slot poll) | materializer, driving `subql`'s `CdcSource` | replication LSN | reconnect with exponential backoff and jitter, resume from the last consumed LSN, alert operators on a bounded outage rather than silently dropping events |
| 2 | Re-execution read against Postgres | materializer, via the engine's async connector | `(query_id, last_observed_lsn)` | [`connetto_server::reexec::ReadFailure`] sorts every failed read into one class by the classification below, and no class reconnects or stops the change stream. `Timeout` ends that subscription at once. `Transient` holds the event, broadcasts `DeliveryPaused { cause: PauseCause::DatabaseUnreachable }`, retries under the shared backoff with a `ReconnectEvent::ReadRetrying` per attempt, and broadcasts `DeliveryResumed` once the read succeeds. `Deterministic` ends that subscription at once, since asking again asks the same question. `Other` retries once after a short wait, and a second failure ends that subscription. An ended subscription gets `NonFatalError` with the `SUBSCRIPTION_REFUSED` phrase, the log line names the class, the attempt count and the cause, and the event is then dispatched to every remaining subscription. |
| 3 | Bootstrap read (initial value for a computed subscription) | materializer | `(sub_id)` | bounded retries, then `Reject` the subscription with the cause and tell the client |
| 4 | Authorization call | materializer, auth filter | request id plus content hash | `OpenFgaPolicy` retries transport failures up to `DEFAULT_CONNECT_RETRIES` (2) times, then fails closed. No patch is delivered and no mutation accepted while the answer is unknown. `ControlMessage::DeliveryPaused { cause: PauseCause::AuthServiceUnreachable }` signals the outage to clients, and `MutationRejectReason::Indeterminate` signals a write refused for the same reason. |
| 5 | Patchset write to a session outbound queue | materializer | server LSN of the batch | per-session bounded retries respecting flow-control credits, back-pressure CDC on session-buffer exhaustion rather than dropping events server-side |
| 6 | Server-side mutation apply (transient Postgres error, serialization failure) | materializer, mutation handler | `client_seq` | A pool checkout failure and a transient failure by the classification below retry with jittered backoff inside [`connetto_server::session::SessionConfig::with_write_retry_budget`] (5 s by default), and the apply reads the durable watermark in its own transaction first, so a commit whose answer was lost is acknowledged rather than applied twice. Past the budget the write is answered `MutationRejectReason::Indeterminate` and every later write of the connection waits behind it. `42501` (`insufficient_privilege`) is answered `MutationRejectReason::Unauthorized`, whether a policy's `WITH CHECK` or a missing grant raised it, and the server log keeps the message that tells the two apart. Any other failure is `MutationReject` with the cause, and the client's optimistic write rolls back. |
| 10 | File chunk transfer | both, per chunk | chunk content hash | per-chunk bounded retries, chunk resumability already covers the multi-chunk case. See [18-file-handling.md](18-file-handling.md). |

The client-side rows, transport reconnect, pending-mutation resend, and local patchset apply, are owned by the client connector and inventoried in [13-client-connection.md](13-client-connection.md).

**Failures are classified by their SQLSTATE.** Rows 2 and 6 and the reconnect log's outage test read one classifier, `diesel_failure` in [reexec.rs](../../crates/connetto-server/src/reexec.rs), so the three never disagree about what an outage is. Where Postgres reported a code, the code alone decides. **Transient** is class `08` (connection exceptions), `40001` (`serialization_failure`), `40P01` (`deadlock_detected`), `25006` (`read_only_sql_transaction`, a standby after failover), `53100` (`disk_full`), `53200` (`out_of_memory`), `53300` (`too_many_connections`), `57P01` to `57P03` (the shutdown codes), `57P05` (`idle_session_timeout`), `58000` (`system_error`) and `58030` (`io_error`). The codes are listed one by one because their classes also hold codes that never clear, such as `40002`, `53400`, `57P04` and `58P01`, which would pause delivery for ever. **Timeout** is `57014`, which connetto raises only through its own `statement_timeout`, and the snapshot path refuses a page read past its limit by the same code. **Deterministic** is every other code. An error carrying no code is classified by its diesel kind instead, where `ClosedConnection`, `UnableToSendCommand`, `SerializationFailure` and `ReadOnlyTransaction` are transient and everything else is **Other**, the one class that keeps a single retry. No classification reads an error message.

### The replication slot

The slot and the publication are the deployment's to provision and to drop, matching the rule that connetto emits no server DDL on any path a deployment runs. Two Postgres facts make the rest necessary. A slot retains WAL without limit by default (`max_slot_wal_keep_size` is `-1`), so a decommissioned or long-crashed connetto-server makes the primary retain its journal until the disk fills and writes stop for everyone. And once the deployment caps it, an invalidated slot leaves a gap in connetto's ingest that sits upstream of the oplog, so the oplog never contains those changes and the stale-cursor comparison cannot see them. What the publication must contain is not only this chapter's question. [08-authorization.md](08-authorization.md) adds that a policy reading a table the publication does not carry also refuses startup, because a permission change the stream never delivers leaves the change-path executor answering from a store that quietly stopped being current. The operator guidance for running the slot, provisioning, capping, watching, and dropping, belongs to [the deployment chapter](20-deployment.md#operating-the-replication-slot).

The change feed reports nothing that distinguishes an invalidated slot from a dropped connection. Every error is stringified and retried alike, and neither `subql` nor `pg_walstream` surfaces a SQLSTATE. So connetto detects the hole rather than the cause. Before opening the stream it reads where the slot will resume from and compares that against how far the reconnect log had already got. Everything the feed delivered was appended to the log before being acknowledged, so an ordinary reconnect resumes at or behind that mark and nothing happens. Ahead of it means changes occurred that the feed never delivered, whatever opened the hole.

A declared epoch does two things, and needs both. The log forgets everything through the resume point, which is retention with a different trigger rather than a new concept, and is what makes the returning client's question answer honestly without a second record to consult and keep in step. And every live connection is closed with `FatalErrorReason::ChangeStreamGap`, because a connection never asks that question again. Reconnecting re-declares its subscriptions through the ordinary path, which rebuilds a running total from its source instead of repairing one that accumulated across the hole.

Several servers sharing one database make the shared log visible. The log is shared, and `append` is written to tolerate several servers writing the same change. One server forgetting a stretch therefore also forgets it for servers that did receive it, whose clients then re-download rather than receiving only what they missed. That is wasted work and never lost data, because re-downloading is always correct.

### Shared retry primitive

`connetto_core` carries the pair. [`connetto_core::backoff::RetryPolicy`] holds one schedule's parameters, 200 ms doubling to a 5 s ceiling by default with optional attempt and total-wait caps and jitter on, and [`connetto_core::backoff::Backoff`] is the episode driver started from it. Each `next_wait` counts an attempt, spreads the wait uniformly over `[wait / 2, wait]`, and answers `None` once a cap ends the episode. The client's reconnect loop, the change-stream reconnect, and the ingest hold arm (an auth outage and a transient computed read alike, each with its own `PauseCause`) all drive it. The browser hub loop in [relay.rs](../../crates/connetto-web/src/relay.rs) doubles its own waits rather than driving the primitive.

The assembled server reports the budget to operators through the same channel. `ingest_with_reconnect` in [session.rs](../../crates/connetto-server/src/session.rs) takes an `on_event` callback, and [`connetto_server::session::ReconnectEvent`] carries `Retrying { attempt, backoff, error }` and `GaveUp { attempts, .. }`, leaving the embedder to wire it to whatever it already runs. A transient computed read reports `ReconnectEvent::ReadRetrying` through the same callback, and a give-up is logged as an error naming that live delivery has stopped. The client-facing give-up stays as well, because the two answer different questions.

### Re-execution and coalescing

The reads are engine-driven. The materializer hosts `subql`'s `AutoResolvingEngine` in async mode, and the engine drives the connector for every database read an event requires. Coalescing is the engine's. It keeps a per-subscription debounce and an in-flight read queue, so a burst of changes to the same subscription collapses into the reads the engine plans, and connetto sets none of the engine's concurrency or debounce knobs. The materializer supplies the setup and the classification. [`connetto_server::reexec::ConnettoReadSetup`] is the per-registration auth context. It carries the budget's `SET LOCAL statement_timeout` and, for a per-viewer registration, the `set_config` statements that bind the caller, so every read the engine issues for that subscription is bounded by the tier of the caller who registered it and answers as that viewer. [`connetto_server::reexec::PgReadConnector`] is `subql`'s shipped async Postgres connector carrying that setup. [`connetto_server::reexec::ReadFailure`] classifies a connector error into `Timeout`, `Transient`, or `Other`. The read runs while the materializer lock is held, which is a measured-risk item whose exit is a non-blocking read-tier drive in `subql`. The contract `subql` owes is that triggers are safely repeatable and `install` is idempotent. Both fall out of the design, since triggers carry no payload and `install` is an unconditional state set.

### Failing closed

- **Authorization failure** is always fail-closed. A row whose auth check could not complete is dropped from delivery, never delivered best effort.
- **CDC source failure** never silently drops events. Outages either resume cleanly (LSN-based) or surface to operators. The client never sees a gap that is not reflected in its resume cursor.
- **Re-execution failure** never stops the change stream. A transient failure pauses delivery and retries the same event, and any other failure ends only the subscription whose read failed while the event still reaches every other subscriber.

### What surfaces to the client

When retries are exhausted, the materializer surfaces one of:

- `MutationReject { client_seq, reason }`: write-path failures.
- `NonFatalError` with the [`connetto_core::messages::SUBSCRIPTION_REFUSED`] phrase, naming the subscription: a computed subscription ended by its re-execution read. The client may subscribe again.
- `SyncFailure { sub_id, reason }`: other read-path failures. The client may re-subscribe or back off.
- `FullResyncRequired { sub_id }`: state unrecoverable via incremental catchup. See [06-reconnect.md](06-reconnect.md).

These are the only give-up surfaces. Everything else is retried inside the materializer without the client knowing.

## subql's reliability contract

For the retry policy above to work, `subql` guarantees:

1. **Triggers are repeatable.** Receiving the same re-execution trigger for a `query_id` twice is safe and not double-counted. The materializer can coalesce or re-emit at will.
2. **`install(query_id, value)` is idempotent.** It unconditionally overwrites the stored value for that query. It does not accumulate or compare.
3. **Event processing is deterministic.** Given the same sequence of CDC events and `install` calls, `subql`'s matching, aggregate maintenance, and conversion produce the same outputs. The I/O lives in the connections the materializer owns and configures (the `CdcSource` and the re-exec connector), and the read scheduling is in-memory state in the engine, deterministic given the same events. Neither is nondeterministic in a way that breaks retry, coalesce, or replay.
4. **No partial state.** `subql` never emits a half-built event. A CDC event is either fully processed (with all resulting notifications and triggers) or not consumed.
5. **No silent drops.** If `subql` cannot process an event (a decode error on a carried cell, for example), it surfaces an error rather than skipping rows.

These five let the materializer treat `subql`'s event processing as a deterministic pure function, so retry, coalesce, and replay are all safe. The retryable I/O sits in the materializer's connections.
