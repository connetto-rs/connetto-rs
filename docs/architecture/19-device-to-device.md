# 19: Device-to-device sync

This chapter covers offline collaboration between devices. It states the certificates a native device enrols with and how they travel between peers, the exactly-once frontier every write settles through, the local-network link and how each platform hosts and joins it, the exchange that moves pending queues between peers, the media that crosses the link for the files the rows name, and the provisional tier those rows live in while the server is away. The decisions with their rejected alternatives are recorded in the plan's [R25](https://github.com/connetto-rs/connetto-rs/blob/82983dff19effa9cfb521370a7794d29e51afb51/plans/master-implementation-plan.md#r25-device-to-device-sync-without-a-server). The clocks the certificate checks run against are [the rule per class](21-clocks.md#the-rule-per-class), the identity and bot list a writer is bound by is [chapter 12's](12-identity-session-capability.md#bots-lists-and-installations), what keeps an applied row on the device is [what covers a row](15-replica-retention.md#what-covers-a-row), and the content a peer pulls over the link is [identity, chunking, and the manifest](18-file-handling.md#identity-chunking-and-the-manifest).

## Purpose

Several people collaborate offline on tasks. The driving case is sample collection in the wild, a team in the Amazon basin with zero internet, mixed iPhone and Android fleets, camera media, and days of irreplaceable observations. Their devices hold overlapping replicas and cannot exchange anything through the server, because every path assumes it, the exactly-once watermark is keyed on a server-minted handle, cursors are positions in the server's change log, and change-path authorization is answered server-side.

## Semantics, provisional and the server adjudicates

What peers build offline is provisional, and the server still adjudicates when connectivity returns. The alternative, a peer-authoritative CRDT mesh with the server as just another peer, was rejected because it replaces server-wins, the single Postgres-authoritative log and the change-path authorization model, and no surveyed system keeps a logical-replication core under one.

Stated so nobody oversells it. An offline edit can be reversed later by the server's verdict. A right the local policy cannot answer blocks the write instead of guessing. Two people editing the same row offline get one locally visible version with the displaced author recorded, one row per key exists, and the server orders the truth later. Nothing here serves collaboration between strangers who were never online together with the same deployment.

## Trust, device identity and certificates

While online and signed in, a native device creates a P-256 key inside its security chip, the Secure Enclave, the Android Keystore or the Windows TPM, which never release it, and a software key in the device's secret store where no usable chip exists. It proves possession with a certificate request carrying a server nonce, and the server's issuer signs an X.509 certificate binding the key to the account. One certificate per account on the device, matching [R42's model](https://github.com/connetto-rs/connetto-rs/blob/82983dff19effa9cfb521370a7794d29e51afb51/plans/master-implementation-plan.md#r42-several-accounts-stay-signed-in-at-once). The deployment's root key stays offline and signs replaceable issuers, and the application is built with the root it expects, so a device never adopts a root it is handed. The server verifies attestation at enrolment where a device offers it and records one of three levels in the certificate, `chip-proven` when Android attests the key in its TEE or StrongBox, `app-attested` when Apple's App Attest vouches for the app that asked, and `unproven` otherwise, and each deployment decides which levels to refuse. The key signs once the device has been unlocked since its last restart, so peers exchange with their screens locked, and the issuer is replaced yearly under a 10-year root. Browsers never enrol.

The lifetime is application-requested per case under a server ceiling and refused if over, the [R4 pattern](https://github.com/connetto-rs/connetto-rs/blob/82983dff19effa9cfb521370a7794d29e51afb51/plans/master-implementation-plan.md#r4-capabilities-in-the-authorization-model). The ceiling defaults to 30 days and is configurable, an unspecified request gets 24 hours, renewal auto-runs on any connectivity past half-life, and a connected client can request a new certificate at another lifetime. There is no human pairing ceremony, because the CA and the policy check replace Syncthing-style fingerprint exchange, and no offline enrolment or sub-issuance, because a server CA cannot mint in the field and delegation is attack surface for marginal gain. A device that never enrolled is a spectator until it meets the server once.

**Lost-device notices travel between peers.** Reporting a device lost revokes its certificates and its sessions, and the issuer publishes a signed X.509 revocation list numbered per issuer. Devices receive lists from the server and hand them to one another inside every authenticated link, keep the highest-numbered list per issuer, and refuse a reported device once any list naming it reaches them. A group no list reaches trusts the certificate until it expires, so lifetime bounds the offline revocation lag. The lost-device list shows a descriptor the application defines, for display only.

**The clock rule and the lost-key path.** Certificate validity is checked against the wall clock with five minutes of tolerance at both ends, the one wall-clock check in the tree a peer cannot correct against a server. A device whose clock puts every certificate outside its window fails closed on every peer link and surfaces one typed event naming the cause, so an expedition device whose clock drifted off-grid is told why it sees no peers. A device whose custody record is gone, by a cleared keychain or a removed store, enrols again with a fresh key exactly as a first enrolment, and the dangling enrolment expires at its lifetime. Provisional rows peers hold under the old key are adjudicated or retracted by the server's frontier like any other author's, since the device cannot sign for a key it no longer holds. On Linux the device key shares the replica key's custody, which survives a reboot ([R71](https://github.com/connetto-rs/connetto-rs/blob/82983dff19effa9cfb521370a7794d29e51afb51/plans/master-implementation-plan.md#r71-linux-replica-key-custody-survives-a-reboot)).

**The device key.** A native build on the platform keyring holds one P-256 key per account, usable once the device was unlocked since its restart and never prompted for. It lives in the Secure Enclave on an iPhone, iPad or Mac whose build carries the keychain entitlement, in the Android Keystore's trusted environment, and in the TPM through the Microsoft Platform Crypto Provider on Windows. A device with no usable chip, which covers Linux, a Mac build without the entitlement and a Windows machine without a usable TPM, keeps a software key as a reserved record in its keyring, outside the unlock gate. An Android application hands in its JNI access with `NativeDurable::with_java_access`, which `connetto_auth_session::java_access()` provides for a Dioxus application, and `NativeClient::device_key_home()` reports where the key lives beside `custody()`. A build whose stores the application names has no device identity.

**Enrolment.** A task beside the client's pump enrols on every connection that finds no certificate, renews past half-life, and serves `NativeClient::reissue_certificate`. It asks for a single-use 60-second nonce, signs a certificate request carrying it on the key's own thread, and sends it with the lifetime and the application's descriptor (`with_certificate_lifetime`, `with_device_descriptor`). The server issues an X509-SVID leaf under its current issuer and records the enrolment and the serial before it answers. A lifetime over the ceiling is refused, and a renewal refused that way is asked again at the ceiling with `ClientEvent::CertificateLifetimeCapped`. The application ships its deployment roots through `with_deployment_roots`, a build with a device identity and no roots does not connect, and every granted chain must reach one of them. The certificate is kept in the replica's `_connetto_device_certificate` and is readable as soon as `connect` returns. Every wait on the server ends after 30 seconds and at once on disconnect.

**Revocation.** The owner reports a device lost from any of the account's sessions with `NativeClient::revoke_device`, and the operator through `SessionManager::revoke_device`. The server lists every serial of the key whose certificate has not been expired longer than `DEVICE_SKEW` ([the rule per class](21-clocks.md#the-rule-per-class)) under the issuer's next CRL Number, closes the device's connections with `FatalErrorReason::DeviceRevoked`, pushes the new lists to every identified session, and revokes the auth-store session its enrolment last ran under through the deployment's session revoker. Every identified session also receives the current lists right after its handshake, and every grant carries them. A client keeps per signer the verified list with the highest number in `_connetto_revocation_list`, ignores a lower one, and logs and ignores an equal number with other content. A device that finds its own serial listed, or is closed as revoked, deletes its key and certificate, wipes its replica and stored credential ([authentication](11-authentication.md)) and emits `ClientEvent::DeviceRevoked`. `NativeClient::devices` returns the account's devices with each descriptor read back as the application's type. Revoking a key that is already revoked repeats every side effect, the published list, the closed connection and the revoked session, so a report whose list failed to publish is completed by asking again.

**Issuer rotation.** A retired issuer stays in the server's configuration (`DeviceCertConfig::with_retired_issuer`) and signs the list of its own certificates until it expires. `connetto-ca revoke-issuer` keeps the root's one complete list of revoked issuers in the CA directory and signs it again under the next number, and the server publishes it beside the issuers' lists (`with_root_list`). A device whose own issuer that list names deletes its certificate, keeps its key, emits `ClientEvent::CertificateWithdrawn`, and enrols the same key under the current issuer on its next connection.

**The enrolment tables.** The server records enrolments in three tables of the deployment's own, named by the `Enrolments` member of its `ConnettoSchema`, one row per device key with its account, session, times and descriptor, one row per issued certificate with its serial, key, issuer and expiry, and one row per issuer holding its last list number, advanced in one atomic upsert so numbers rise across restarts and server instances. The descriptor's columns map the application's own type, which `connetto_schema!` destructures field by field, so a field list that disagrees with it does not compile, and an enrolment whose descriptor does not decode into it is refused with `InvalidRequest`. `ServerBuilder::device_identity` turns enrolment on, requires the three tables at startup and revokes a lost device's session in the auth store. The binary reads the issuer from the directory `connetto-ca issuer` wrote ([deployment](20-deployment.md)).

**The clock rule on the client and the lost-key path.** A client learns its clock is wrong from its own certificate. A certificate the server just granted that the local clock already puts outside its window, or one held at open that is not yet valid by more than five minutes, moves it to `ClockOff` and raises `ClientEvent::ClockOutsideWindow { ahead }` once, the server's issuance serving as the trusted time. While `ClockOff` the hourly look re-evaluates the window and never renews, and a connection still renews once, which leaves the state when the new certificate falls inside the window. A device whose key record is gone opens a fresh key, drops the certificate of the lost one and enrols the fresh key as a first enrolment, and the old enrolment stays until it expires.

**Attestation.** A device's first enrolment carries its platform's evidence, and the server records one of three levels in the enrolment table and in a non-critical certificate extension, which renewals keep. An Android key is created with the fixed attestation challenge `connetto device key` and sends its Keystore chain, which the server checks against Google's attestation roots and Google's attestation status list, recording `chip-proven` for a `TrustedEnvironment` or `StrongBox` key. A chain naming another key or challenge is refused, and every other shortfall, the emulator's software-rooted chain among them, records `unproven`. An iPhone or iPad asks App Attest to sign over the request's hash through `connetto-app-attest`, the workspace's one crate allowed `unsafe`, which serialises every App Attest call behind one process-wide lock since Apple's first call is not thread-safe, and the server checks it against Apple's root and the App IDs the deployment lists, recording `app-attested`. Windows, Linux and a Mac record `unproven`. A deployment names the levels it accepts, all three by default, and refuses the rest with `AttestationRequired`, which the client raises as `ClientEvent::AttestationRequired` and asks again only on its next connection.

**The attestations on hardware.** An iPhone 15 Pro Max enrols as `app-attested` on hardware, with a real attestation from it checked in as the verifier's fixture, a Wi-Fi iPad A16 enrolled as `app-attested` against a server accepting no lower level, and a Linux client enrolled as `unproven`.

**The bot list in the certificate.** Every certificate carries a non-critical extension under `1.3.6.1.4.1.67105.2`, beside the attestation extension's `1.3.6.1.4.1.67105.1`, whose value is the DER `UTF8String` of the bot list text the enrolling session's connection binds as `app.bot_list`, the wildcard `*` for a person and the list for a bot. A certificate without the extension carries none, which a reader binds as `NULL` so a certificate that does not say fails closed. The server writes it at first enrolment, at renewal and at an online reissue from the current session's caller, so a changed list reaches its peers when the certificate renews.

## The per-device applied frontier

Every write carries its durable identity, the pair of its writer and its pending-queue sequence. A native device with an enrolled key writes as that key, and every other client, a browser profile, a build without a device identity, a device before its first enrolment, a caller with no identity, writes under a number the server issues, since browsers never join a peer link and no browser API offers an application a chip-held signing key. A connection proves its device writer once, by signing the nonce the server opens every connection with. The server keeps one frontier per writer below which every sequence is settled, applied or refused, and every path takes only the next sequence. The normal reconnect path, the courier path and an archive restore all settle through it, and the per-connection watermark is a cache of it.

The server keeps a record of each settled write, its chain hash, its outcome and the path that settled it, for as long as its writer can still deliver it. A device writer's records go once its enrolment is revoked or has gone absent, an expired certificate keeping them behind a same-key renewal for the replay and fork evidence, and a number writer's once the author reports it has settled past them. A re-delivery is answered from that record, and audited when it arrives through another path than the one that settled it. A different entry at a settled (writer, sequence) is refused and audited, first-seen-wins, the Secure Scuttlebutt fork rule. An archive import therefore always retires the archived writer and gives the importing replica a fresh one, with restored entries carrying their original identity as provenance so the frontier deduplicates the logical writes across the restore. A device whose writer an import retired carries its own unsent entries forward the same way. The pending queue holds every unsent write and evicts none, so every provisional copy a peer holds stays confirmable or retractable, and every entry chains to the one before it, so a range a device signs vouches for everything below it.

Outstanding writes have durable append positions across writers. Reapplication and archive export follow those positions, which carries preserve. Older number and unissued tails drain before device binding, and the archive's pending list preserves their causal order.

Device retirement commits the carried tail and a durable key-exclusion marker together before custody cleanup and fresh enrolment. Device revocation uses the same queue transaction and then deletes custody through its revocation lifecycle, without requesting writer-retirement enrolment. A held or renewed certificate cannot reactivate an excluded writer.

## Transport

The data plane is the local network, with discovery by gateway-probe first, a hotspot host is the gateway, so clients need no multicast to find it, and mDNS as the general case under the service type `_connetto-peer._tcp.local`, then mutual TLS over plain TCP with both sides verifying against the built-in roots and the revocation lists. It is for native targets only, phones, tablets, laptops and desktops alike, since browsers cannot take the listening role on any radio path and keep the server path. The TXT record carries the protocol version and a certificate fingerprint only, never an account identity, and discovery is treated as fully public with authentication happening only at the handshake.

**The link.** The `connetto-peer` crate holds the link, and `connetto-client`'s `peer` feature drives it from the enrolment task. A device holding a valid certificate listens at the address `NativeDurable::with_peer_listener` names, `0.0.0.0:0` by default, and dials with `NativeClient::link_peer`. Both sides run TLS 1.3 signed by their device keys, verify each other against the deployment roots, the kept revocation lists, the five-minute window and the accepted attestation levels, and then exchange the lists the other lacks through the same intake the server's pushes use, so a list learned from a peer revokes exactly as one pushed by the server. A link pings every 15 seconds, closes after 45 seconds of silence or when the peer's certificate expires, and both sides see the close.

**Discovery.** While a device serves, it advertises `_connetto-peer._tcp.local` over mDNS under a random name, with only the frame version and the SHA-256 of its current certificate in the TXT record, and browses for the others, reporting each as `ClientEvent::PeerFound` and `PeerGone`. Unless the application turns autolink off with `NativeDurable::with_peer_autolink(false)`, the client dials what it finds, retrying an unreachable peer from 5 seconds up to 5 minutes and never redialling a refused, revoked or expired one until it advertises a renewed certificate. At most 8 dials run at once and at most 256 found devices are tracked, so fake advertisements cannot exhaust the device. Android holds a multicast lock while browsing. A device that finds a host it already links to names it in the same event stream.

**The peer link switch.** `NativeClient::set_peer_links` steers every peer link path at run time, and `NativeClient::peer_links_on` answers its standing, which is always on for a build without a device identity. Off refuses every kind of peer link, the discovery dials for peers on the same network, the dial after a hotspot join and its redials, an explicit `NativeClient::link_peer`, which answers `PeerError::LinksOff` before a socket opens, and an incoming link, which completes its handshake and is refused before it registers, and it closes every live link with the close frame the protocol already carries, `CloseReason::Closed`, so the wire format stays as it is and the other device redials on its backoff. The Wi-Fi itself still joins and hosts while the links are off. The standing lasts for the running client, every start beginning with links on, and a certificate renewal's re-serve keeps it on the same node, as do an expiry's stop and a clock's. Coming back on, every found peer's backoff resets and its dial starts at once, the barred peers staying barred, and a joined hotspot whose gateway and port are known starts its dial with a fresh bound.

**Hosting and joining on Android.** `NativeClient::host_hotspot` starts Android's local-only hotspot and answers its SSID, passphrase and, while the device serves, its peer port. `join_hotspot` asks the platform for that network, which the user approves, binds only the peer sockets to it so the server connection keeps the phone's usual network, and dials the host at the gateway on the offered port. The hotspot follows the application's calls and the client's life, not the certificate, so an expired host keeps its group's network up. The Kotlin these need ships in `connetto-peer-android`, and every system whose slice has not landed answers `Unsupported`. Hosting and joining were proven on two A35s, the Android 15 phone hosting and the Android 14 phone joining and linking, and an A35 starts its access point only once its own Wi-Fi is off, measured while that Wi-Fi sat on eduroam's 5 GHz channel 56.

**The state reads.** The client's event stream is broadcast, so an application that keeps a list from its events holds a wrong one after any dropped event. The node's read [`connetto_peer::Node::linked`] stands the identities of every peer it reports linked, live slots and a duplicate close's held peer alike, and `NativeClient::linked_peers` hands them to the application, an empty list for a build without a device. `NativeClient::nearby_hosts` stands the scan's hosts as `NearbyHost` values with their host key, beacon prefix and last signal, published whenever a host is seen, refreshed, renamed or gone, and the test harness's Linux leg reads both instead of keeping its own standing.

**Making a shared network and joining it.** Android and Linux start a hotspot from the application, Android's local-only hotspot and NetworkManager's hotspot, both with no SIM and no internet. Apple devices only join. macOS cannot host from an application, and an iPhone hosts only through Personal Hotspot, which Apple builds to share the phone's mobile data, greys out with mobile data off, and leaves an application unable to start or read. A group whose mobile data works reaches the server, so an iPhone host would serve no group the peer link exists for (R77 decision 23). A host announces itself with a public Bluetooth beacon carrying the protocol version and its certificate fingerprint. A joining device connects to it over Bluetooth, both run the same mutual TLS as the Wi-Fi link with the same checks and exchange revocation lists, and only then does the host send the hotspot's name and password, encrypted for that device, as Apple's Wi-Fi password sharing proves identity before sending a password. With Bluetooth off, the host shows a `WIFI:` QR code and the same details as text. Every platform then asks its own system to join.

The decision rests on the platform behaviour it works with. A hotspot host is a member of its own network, Android at `192.168.x.1` through `LocalOnlyHotspot`, application-startable with no SIM service. Android multicast reception needs a `MulticastLock`, and iOS 14 and later and macOS 15 and later prompt once for local-network permission. A university network can put devices in separate subnets that mDNS cannot cross, measured on eduroam, and a battery travel router removes every hotspot caveat. Bluetooth carries only the hotspot handoff. A full Bluetooth data plane is never promised, the crate landscape being pre-1.0 and single-maintainer, and Noise returns to the cipher table only if that plane ever exists. A group of Apple devices alone, with no Android phone, no Linux machine and no shared Wi-Fi, has no link, and R112 brings Apple's peer-to-peer Wi-Fi for it.

**The beacon and the exchange.** The `bluetooth` feature of `connetto-peer` advertises the beacon while the device both hosts a hotspot and serves its peer link. The beacon codec ([beacon.rs](../../crates/connetto-peer/src/beacon.rs)) carries the frame version and the fingerprint of the device's current certificate in a legacy advertisement, and the framed exchange ([exchange.rs](../../crates/connetto-peer/src/exchange.rs)) runs the link's TLS 1.3 with the same verifier over a GATT service under connetto's service UUID, one characteristic the joiner writes and one it reads notifications from, the frames split to the negotiated packet size. Every exchange is bounded at 30 seconds, at most 4 run at once on a host, and a fifth connection is refused at once. The host swaps `Hello` frames and the lists either side lacks into the one intake and only then does its `Node::offer_over` send the `HotspotOffer` with its peer port, and the joiner's `NativeClient::fetch_offer` reads the same offer. On Android the host runs `BluetoothGattServer`. The exchange and both state machines were proven over in-memory transports, and one A35 hosted with its beacon visible in its own Bluetooth dump.

**The join side.** `connetto-client` runs btleplug on one dedicated thread as its central (`BtleplugCentral` in [central.rs](../../crates/connetto-client/src/bluetooth/central.rs)), attached to the application's class loader, with btleplug's Java in `connetto-peer-android`'s Gradle module and `java_vm` building the running virtual machine from the one manganis hands out. `join_nearby` runs the exchange with a found host and hands the offer to the platform's joiner, and `fetch_offer` asks a named host for its offer without joining. The Android 14 one of two A35s saw the Android 15 one's beacon, joined through the demo's `Join the nearby host`, the exchange handing over the offer and the approval, and both phones named each other linked.

**The text and QR fallback.** `HotspotOffer::to_wifi_payload` writes the standard `WIFI:` fields with the peer port in the extension field, `WIFI:T:<WPA or SAE>;S:<ssid>;P:<password>;X:<port>;;` with the format's backslash escapes, and `from_wifi_payload` reads it with a typed refusal (`WifiPayloadError`), so a phone's system camera joins the Wi-Fi and skips the port while an application that scans the code or takes the string learns the port and dials the gateway at once. The offer's three lines beside the payload are the text form. The fallback was proven with the host's Bluetooth off, the Android 15 one hosting and the Android 14 one joining from the pasted payload and linking.

**The iPhone and the iPad join.** `connetto-peer-apple` bridges `NEHotspotConfiguration` and reads the app's Bluetooth permission from `CBManager.authorization`, a class property that needs no manager. `connetto-client` adds `AppleHotspotBackend` and `CentralReadiness`, and the btleplug central runs over CoreBluetooth. No Apple device runs a peripheral, so the central is the only Bluetooth manager. It starts once the permission allows it, or once a call the user made asks for the permission, since btleplug's `CBCentralManager` is what asks. The power comes from btleplug's adapter state and its state events, and a call made with the radio off restarts the central so iOS shows its power alert again. An Apple joiner applies its configuration once more when the system answers success but the interface has not moved, once per join within the join bound. iOS auto-join can take an application-joined device back to a known network within seconds of the join. In one three-device run the iPad left an iPhone's hotspot for the home Wi-Fi twice, once through `trigger=known_network_added` and once through `trigger=retry (ac), mode=best`, so a joined network that moves elsewhere reads as lost. R76 slice 9f's star proved the iPhone 15 Pro Max and the iPad A16 joining the Android phone's local-only hotspot by beacon, each linking to every other device over it.

**Hosting and joining on Linux.** `connetto-peer`'s Linux module drives NetworkManager over zbus and runs the beacon's GATT service over BlueZ through `bluer`, and `connetto-client` adds `LinuxHotspotBackend` and `LinuxBluetoothBackend` beside the btleplug central. A Linux host names its network `connetto-` and four random hex digits with a fresh passphrase held in memory, tries 5 GHz for 10 seconds before 2.4 GHz, and one Wi-Fi device refuses the second of hosting and joining with `HotspotError::Joined` or `JoinError::Hosting`. A join is a volatile NetworkManager profile its user owns, which needs `network-control` and `settings.modify.own`, and hosting also needs `wifi.share.protected`. The Linux and Android legs passed all six joins in one unattended run, each hosting in turn while the other joined by the beacon, the pasted payload and the typed form.

**The Mac join.** `connetto-peer-apple` joins on macOS through CoreWLAN, scanning for the offered network every 5 seconds for up to 30 seconds, since a freshly started hotspot can be absent from the first scans and a scan soon after another can fail. macOS hands a process without Location Services access scanned networks with no name, so a Mac join answers `JoinError::MissingPermission("Location Services")` until the user allows it through `connetto_peer_apple::hotspot::request_location_access()`. The Mac never hosts. A headless client on a Mac joined an Android phone's local-only hotspot, hosted from the Tauri demo, by the typed form, the Bluetooth beacon and the pasted payload in one unattended run.

## The exchange

Each device offers its own pending queue, a hash chain whose newest entry its device key signs at each hand-off. The exchange is direct only, no relay and no gossip, because a camp group on one network is small and fully pairwise. Per peer, a device keeps the highest sequence received, and an exchange is a mutual offer of queue bounds plus the acked frontier plus the retraction log, a request for the range from cursor plus one to the offer's newest sequence, signed changesets streamed in order, and the cursor advanced in the same transaction as the apply. Each entry's hash covers its capture major and its provenance as well as its predecessor, its sequence and its changeset, so one signature over a head vouches for every major and original writer below it, for a peer and a courier alike. One signature covers the whole offer, and a receiver verifies the chain from the offered range's first entry to the signed head before it applies anything. An entry captured under another application schema build than the receiver's crosses as its hash alone and is never applied.

**The exchange frames on the link.** The link speaks frame version 3, which the hello, the mDNS TXT record and the Bluetooth beacon carry, so a device of another version is refused at hello. One [`connetto_peer::PeerFrame::Exchange`] frame carries the offer, the request, the manifest, the body or the end, and a manifest holds at most [`connetto_exchange::MAX_MANIFEST_ROWS`] rows, refused above the bound on both the write and the read. `Node::send_exchange` queues a frame on one peer's link through a bounded queue the sender awaits, so a sender cannot pile frames up in memory, refuses a peer with no live link, and hands the frame back when the link closes while it waits. A received frame reaches the node's owner as `PeerEvent::Exchange` naming its peer, and the client's enrolment task, which holds the device key, feeds it to the exchange driver in [exchange.rs](../../crates/connetto-client/src/enrolment/task/exchange.rs).

**The media frames on the link.** One [`connetto_peer::PeerFrame::Media`] frame carries a [`connetto_exchange::media::Media`] message, a `Want` for a file's manifest, a `Manifest` run of at most [`connetto_exchange::MAX_MANIFEST_ROWS`] rows, a `Fetch` of at most [`connetto_exchange::media::MAX_FETCH_LEN`] bytes of a file, a `Piece` of at most [`connetto_exchange::media::MAX_PIECE_BYTES`] encoded bytes, or a `Refused` or `Busy` answer, each bound refused on both the write and the read. `Node::send_media` queues it on the link's own bounded queue, eight frames deep beside the exchange's eight, and the link writes a queued exchange frame first, so an offer never waits behind a chunk's pieces. [`connetto_exchange::media::MediaLinks`] keeps the per-peer state both roles need without I/O, one request outstanding each way per peer with every reply quoting its id, on-demand asks queued ahead of prefetches, a holder serving at most two peers at once and answering `Busy` beyond them, and the links in the order they came up, the order a receiver tries holders in.

**The media port.** A received media frame reaches the enrolment task as `PeerEvent::Media`, which hands it, with every link and unlink and the bot list each linked certificate carries, to the one `MediaPort` the application took with `NativeClient::media_port`. While no port lives, a request is answered `Refused` at once, the answer a holder gives for a file it lacks. A holder judges a requester with `ConnettoConnection::peer_may_read`, which binds the requester's account, `NULL` subjects and its certificate's bot list as an author's are bound for a peer apply, asks the policy view whether any row of the declared table names the file in the declared column, and binds the device's own identity again. A build with no caller mapping refuses every requester, and a certificate without a bot list reads nothing a bot cap guards. `ConnettoConnection::provisional_blobs` answers the file each still provisional row names in a declared column, with the record's author. All three exist in a build with the `peer` feature, in [media.rs](../../crates/connetto-client/src/media.rs) and on the connection.

**The protocol.** The messages and the state machine of both roles live in [`connetto_exchange`], which reads no database, opens no socket and starts no timer, so the driver feeds it link events, messages and the monotonic clock and carries out the [`connetto_exchange::Action`]s it returns. A request names its whole range and the requester's build, so an entry the author writes after signing never reaches a manifest checked against the older head, and the sender sends a body only for an entry captured under that build. A range starting at or below the author's settled frontier has already left its queue and is answered `NotAvailable`, kept apart from `Busy`, and the receiver acts on the author's next offer, which every settlement produces. A receiver keeps one verified sequence and head per author, its cursor, and an offer whose settled or newest head contradicts the cursor at its sequence raises `PeerChainInvalid`. A fork below the cursor is left to the server's chain check at settlement. A stalled request is retried from both ends. An author that answered `Busy` offers again once its stream ends, and a receiver that waited [`connetto_exchange::FRAME_BOUND`] for the next frame requests again after a backoff from [`connetto_exchange::RETRY_FIRST`] doubling to [`connetto_exchange::RETRY_CEILING`]. No existing exchange library fits, because each signs with ed25519, which the Secure Enclave and StrongBox cannot hold.

**The chain and the attestation.** The entry hash, the domain `connetto-chain-v2`, covers the entry's capture major, its provenance, its predecessor, its sequence and its changeset, and both the client's queue and the server's settle path compute it, so a direct write carries no hash on the wire. The device key signs one offer per hand-off, over the settled and newest frontiers, the build list and the retraction log, as [`connetto_core::chain::offer_message`] encodes it and [`connetto_core::chain::verify_offer`] checks it, and a manifest's rows recompute the chain from a verified head to the offer's newest head through [`connetto_core::chain::chain_walk`]. The one offer serves the peer and the courier alike, so neither path signs anything else.

Cleared sequences need no tombstones, with one repair. An acked row reaches a peer through ordinary subscription delivery only where the peer's subscriptions cover it, so each exchange carries the author's acked frontier, and provisional rows at or below it are marked adjudicated and fall under ordinary coverage. Refusal keeps its own signal, a signed retraction log of refused sequences with their `MutationRejectReason`, and a retraction removes the peer's provisional copy. The author is authoritative over its own queue's fate. A receiver that never meets the author again asks the server instead on every connection, presenting the author's signed offer as its right to ask ([R77](https://github.com/connetto-rs/connetto-rs/blob/729d2f2321d0e8866472f8dcab8455715243d1a2/plans/master-implementation-plan.md#r77-the-exchange-and-the-provisional-tier) decision 11). The server answers each sequence from its settled records, and a sequence that answers `Pending` stays an open question for the rest of the connection, so the server pushes its verdict the moment it settles, in a further `ProvisionalVerdicts` naming the ask's own request id, the wire gaining no message. It keeps a refusal past its prune, so a refused write never reads as settled, and an author revoked, retired or expired past the tolerance answers abandoned above its frontier. A resync keeps provisional rows the way it keeps an unsent write.

Offline authorization is the translated replica policy evaluated with the author's identity bound, through a rebindable evaluator, failing closed on OpenFGA-derived and locally unanswerable rights. Two limits are part of the contract, the answer is computed on the receiver's partial replica, so an absent membership row produces a false deny (fail closed, accepted), and an offline-stale replica can produce a bounded false allow, the same staleness class as certificate revocation lag. The receiver replays the author's write through the policy views on its own connection with the author bound, the author's share keys unknown and so absent, and the author's bot list taken from the certificate. Every certificate carries the bot list the server binds for its enrolling session, the wildcard for a person and the list for a bot, so bots on field devices can exchange too, and a certificate without it fails closed. A list that changes reaches peers when the certificate renews, within the same bounded false allow. An author's write applies whole or not at all, and a peer's change to a row the receiver never held is refused, since a changeset's update carries only the changed columns.

## The provisional tier

Peer-applied rows live in the ordinary replica tables so the application's own SQL sees them, with provenance beside them keyed by table and key, author identity, author device, received-at, state. The provenance, the per-author cursors and the applied entries live beside the pending queue in the replica's main database, never in the attached device tier, because the rows, their provenance and the cursor commit together and SQLite keeps a WAL transaction atomic only within each database file. Peer applies suspend capture exactly as server patches do, or a peer's write would re-upload as the receiver's own. A provisional row is held by the reserved holder `connetto-provisional` while its record is provisional, so the eviction passes keep it as they keep any held row. The conflict rule is explicit, the receiver's own unsent write is never displaced, any other collision applies last-writer-locally with the displaced author recorded, and server-wins stays reserved for server patches. Server-sourced data writing a key clears its provenance record. The client exposes typed provenance reads and applied, confirmed and retracted-with-cause events, and presentation is the application's.

**The provenance bookkeeping.** A build with the `peer` feature keeps `_connetto_provisional` beside the pending queue in the replica's main database ([provisional.rs](../../crates/connetto-client/src/provisional.rs)), one record per peer-applied row keyed by its physical table and primary key, naming the author's key, the queue sequence, the author's account, the received-at time, the state and any displaced author. The eviction passes keep a row whose record is still provisional through the record's `connetto-provisional` holding ([holdings.rs](../../crates/connetto-client/src/holdings.rs)), written and released with the record in the same transaction, in the store's holdings under this identity for a shared table. A server patch or snapshot writing such a key deletes its record in the same transaction and raises one `ClientEvent::ProvisionalConfirmed` per apply naming every record it cleared, except a resync's replacement snapshot, which is no verdict and leaves the record and its holding for the re-apply at the replacement's end. A truncate or full resync that takes a row takes its record with it, so a record never outlives its row. `NativeClient::provenance` and `provisional_rows` read the records back.

**The peer apply.** A build with the `peer` feature applies a verified peer entry as its author, the entry's ops running through the policy views with the author's caller, subject and bot-list functions bound for the life of the apply and the receiver's own restored on the way out, under `UnrecordedWrites`, so the outbox records none of the author's rows and they never upload as the receiver's. The receiver's writes still in the outbox are queued first, so every unsent write holds its key under a writer and sequence, and those keys are filtered, the author recorded displaced by the write, and every landed key keeps one provisional record beside its row. The entry, the author's bot list among it, and its records land in one transaction at a replica-wide append position the next entry takes, and the apply raises one `ClientEvent::ProvisionalApplied` naming every record it wrote. An op lands when the backing row stands as the op left it afterwards, since SQLite counts no change through a view's trigger. Every key is recorded in key order, the order server writes and `provenance` name it in. A table the build shares is written through its view in the store, as a server patch is, while its record stays in this identity's own replica, so another identity on the device reads the row before the server rules on it, as it reads this identity's unsent shared writes. A guard's refusal, recognised by pg2sqlite's `is_policy_refusal`, an op that leaves the backing row otherwise, or a constraint failure refuses the entry whole, keeps its body courier-only and raises one `ClientEvent::PeerWriteRefused` naming the entry's rows. An op that names its row by no key refuses the entry whole the same way, as `PeerRefusal::Unkeyed`, since no synced table lacks a key and such an op can only come from a crafted body. A body whose digest disagrees with the manifest row is refused before the views and kept nowhere, and an entry captured under another major is skipped and kept digest-only, its manifest row alone. Records and events name each table as the application writes it. A provisional record stays until its verdict even when its row is gone, so a retraction can restore a provisional delete, and only an adjudicated record goes with its row. A retraction inverts the stored entry's ops, stands a displaced author's provenance back from its stored entry or keeps no record of one that is gone, and raises one `ClientEvent::ProvisionalRetracted` naming the keys it inverted, a retraction of an entry the receiver never stored raising nothing. An undo the store refuses or skips fails the retraction whole, every row, record and the entry staying as they were, so a later retraction can try again. A frontier adjudication moves the author's settled records to adjudicated and retains the entries.

**The courier hold.** `_connetto_peer_entry` ([peer.rs](../../crates/connetto-client/src/peer.rs)) keeps every entry a verified range carried, with its sequence, capture major, origin and digest, in one of four states. A provisional entry applied with its records. A courier-only entry is an own-build body the receiver's policies refused, never applied. A digest-only entry is another build's, kept without a body. A retained entry was adjudicated and keeps its body and row, so a courier can bring it back to a server restored from an earlier backup. The author's frontier over a link and a server verdict of `Applied` or `Settled` retain every held entry they reach, a provisional one's records moving to adjudicated first, and a retraction, a `Refused` verdict or an `Abandoned` one inverts and deletes a provisional entry and deletes a courier-only or digest-only one. The session's provisional asks cover every held entry but the retained. A resync's re-apply takes the provisional entries alone. R31's rebuild at a newer major keeps every held entry under its major and holds a provisional one courier-only, its record going with the synced tables. Each author's cursor keeps `expires_at`, the end of the author's certificate as `PeerEvent::Linked` reported it, moved by `PeerEvent::Renewed` when the link presents a renewed certificate. A cursor an earlier build stored, whose author's end the receiver never learned, counts it as 30 days after the upgrade. A retained entry goes once its author's end is more than `DEVICE_SKEW` past, or once its author has no cursor, since a courier delivers nothing without the author's stored offer, checked at each open, link adoption and adjudication.

**Aggregates do not follow.** Server-computed aggregate values (`LiveValue`) are pushed by the server and freeze while offline, so they reflect neither the device's own unsent writes nor peer-applied provisional rows, and they catch up only when connectivity returns and the server has adjudicated. An application showing counts or sums over provisional data derives them from the rows with its own local queries, which see the provisional tier by construction.

**The handheld run.** Three accounts on three phones, alice on an iPhone, bob on an iPad and carol on an Android phone hosting the hotspot, wrote one team note each with the server stopped, and every phone showed the other two notes provisional over the peer link, the tidy pass leaving them standing. With the links switched off and the hotspot left, the operator removed carol from the team and the server came back. Bob's note was confirmed on alice's phone and alice's on bob's, carol's note was rolled back on all three phones because unauthorized, and alice's and bob's notes left carol's phone with her membership, in one unattended run of the test harness.

## The courier path

A certified bearer relays another device's signed work to the server, so a lost device's entries reach it from the camp. The server accepts couriers only where the deployment turns the path on, `ServerBuilder::courier(true)` or `CONNETTO_COURIER=on`, and every [`connetto_core::messages::HandshakeAck`] carries `courier` so a client knows before uploading anything. A connection that bound its own device writer opens a [`connetto_core::messages::CourierRange`] with the author's stored signed offer, the manifest rows it holds up to the offer's newest entry and the number of [`connetto_core::messages::CourierBody`] frames that follow, one range open per connection. The server refuses the range before reading anything when the path is off or the connection bound no device writer, and before settling anything when the rows do not hold together, the author's enrolment is absent, revoked or past its expiry plus `DEVICE_SKEW`, the author's account is banned, its writer is retired, the offer's signature fails, or the rows above the writer's frontier skip past it or do not chain from the writer's head to the offer's newest head. Rows at or below the frontier are answered with the verdict a provisional ask gives. Each body then settles as it lands, under the author's account, the bot list its certificate carries (kept in the enrolment table's `bot_list`) and no subjects, through the write path a direct upload takes. The range stops at a row with no body, of another major or with a number original, at a row that settles nothing, and at a sequence another path settled under a different entry, and its remaining bodies are dropped. One [`connetto_core::messages::CourierAnswer`] follows the last counted body. A body of another range, naming no row of it, out of order, unreadable or off its row's digest ends the connection, the entries settled before it standing. [courier.rs](../../crates/connetto-server/src/courier.rs) holds the range's state.

Every settle of a couriered entry writes its one `write_couriered` audit row naming the author's account in `user_id` and the courier's account in `courier_user_id`, under the courier's session, so the row says whose entries landed and who carried them. The same settle writes one notice row in `_connetto_courier_notices`, in the settle's own transaction, naming the author's account, the courier's account, the author's writer key, the sequence and the stored outcome, so a notice row and its settle stand or fall together. The author's next identified handshake carries its unacked notice rows as [`connetto_core::messages::CourierNotice`] frames, one frame per chunk of the manifest cap under its own id, and the [`connetto_core::messages::CourierNoticeAck`] a client sends after a frame deletes only that frame's rows, so a row outlives a connection until an ack takes it and a later handshake carries it again. A courier connection draws on its own byte budget, the per-minute window [`connetto_server::throttle::ThrottleConfig::with_courier_bytes`] sets beside the caller's write budget, and a body that draws past it answers `Retry` with its wait, or `NotCouriered` where the body is wider than the whole window, and the range drains its remaining bodies.

## Media over the link

**What crosses.** The exchange moves the rows, and a row whose declared column names a file id can move its bytes over the same link, pulled by the receiver one chunk at a time, every piece verified against the file id as it arrives. The messages, the bounds each one obeys and the per-peer state the links keep without I/O stand under [The exchange](#the-exchange) beside the exchange's own frames, on their own queue the link writes after its queued exchange frames, and the one port the enrolment task hands them to stands there as well. This section states the gate a holder runs on a request, the verification a receiver runs on a piece, and the life the fetched bytes live on the receiver.

**The gate.** The application declares every column that holds a file id with `with_peer_content`, the same declaration in every build of the application, and awaiting one the replica does not hold is refused naming the missing table or column. A holder asked for a file binds the requester's certificate account, `NULL` subjects and the certificate's bot list on its own connection for one statement, asks the policy view whether any row of any declared column names the file, and serves when any answers a row. It refuses when nothing is declared, when the build has no caller mapping and so no policy view, and when a statement fails, so the gate fails closed on what it cannot answer, and it judges every request with the bot list the certificate carries now, so a renewed certificate changes what its holder reads. `ConnettoConnection::peer_may_read` is the statement and `ConnettoConnection::provisional_blobs` answers the value each still provisional row of a declared column holds with its record's author, which the candidate order and the spare below both read.

**The holder's stream.** With the gate admitting the requester, a `Want` streams the file's manifest rows in runs of at most [`connetto_exchange::MAX_MANIFEST_ROWS`] rows, and a `Fetch` asks for one of the file's chunks by the chunk's byte range in the file, since two holders can chunk one file differently, the author under its mime class and a server-fetched copy under the generic class, while a byte range of the file is the same on every holder, and streams the range as the verified encoding of [`connetto_file_core::encode_range`] in pieces of at most [`connetto_exchange::media::MAX_PIECE_BYTES`] encoded bytes, the outboard computed from the stored chunks when its object is missing. The holder answers `Refused` alike for a file it lacks and a requester its gate refuses, the rule the file server keeps by answering `404` for both. A peer is served one request at a time and at most two peers at once, each serve holding one decrypted chunk, and a request beyond that is answered `Busy`. A stream stops when its peer's link falls and answers `Refused` when the file is evicted or a chunk cannot be read partway through.

**The receiver's candidate order.** A fetch asks the linked authors of the provisional rows whose declared columns name the file, in the order their rows name it, then every other linked peer in the order its link came up, so a teammate who synced the row and its bytes before camp serves them while the author is out of range. It moves to the next candidate on a `Refused`, a `Busy`, 30 seconds of silence on the link or a failed verification, and a candidate whose link falls is dropped with the ask it owed.

**Chunk verification.** Every piece verifies against the file id as it arrives, [`connetto_file_core::decode_range`] reading the encoding one chunk at a time with one chunk in memory, and a piece that fails it, or a finished chunk that does not hash to its manifest row, aborts the fetch and drops the holder's manifest with the chunk's pieces, the next candidate starting again at the `Want`. A verified chunk is written under its own hash through the receiver's [`connetto_file_core::EncryptingStore`], re-encrypted under its own key with a fresh nonce, and once every chunk is in the store the manifest rows and the outboard row commit in one transaction with no outbox row, which is what makes the fetched bytes cache, never exported and evictable, and the commit raises one [`connetto_file_client::ContentEvent`] `Fetched`.

**Resume.** The manifest a holder sends holds the holder's own chunk boundaries, and a fetch that restarts asks for the first chunk the receiver's store lacks and skips the rest. A lost link mid-chunk drops that chunk's verified pieces and keeps the earlier chunks.

**The on-demand bound.** A fetch every remaining candidate answered `Busy` asks them again after 5 seconds doubling to 5 minutes, a link coming up waking it at once, and answers `Unavailable` 30 seconds after it began or after its last verified chunk, so an on-demand fetch the holders cannot serve ends rather than waits.

**The resolve stage.** [`connetto_file_client::ContentClient::resolve`] answers locally for every file whose chunks the device holds, whether unsent, pinned or cached, asks the server for a signed URL otherwise, and with the `peer` feature and a running media driver turns to a linked holder while offline or when the server refuses the ticket, the case of a provisional row whose author has not uploaded yet. The fetched chunks land in the chunk store, whose source answers for them, so the local source list the application extends keeps its local sources alone. With no driver running or no candidate left, the file answers `Unavailable`.

**The provisional spare.** [`connetto_file_client::ContentClient`] `tidy_content` keeps a manifest while the file is unsent or a pin names it, and with the `peer` feature also while a row whose peer apply still awaits a verdict names it in a declared column, so a photo pulled in camp survives the next crossing while the server is away. Adjudication or a server write on the row makes the file ordinary cache, which the next pass removes and the server serves once its author has uploaded it, so a receiver online before that upload reads `Unavailable` until it lands, and a file fetched for a row that was never provisional, from a teammate's synced copy, is ordinary cache from the moment it lands.

**The retention triggers.** The outbox driver runs the retention pass once at its start and on every `ProvisionalRetracted`, so the bytes of a write the server or its author refused leave the receiver at once, and a retraction the event broadcast dropped is caught at the next start. The pass is the whole retention pass, so it also removes ordinary cache, which a linked holder can serve again.

**The thumbnail prefetch.** `prefetch` on a `with_peer_content` declaration marks a thumbnail column. A receiver fetches each file a prefetch column of the author's provisional rows names that it lacks from that author, when the author's entry applies and when the author's link comes up, the second reading the records since the event broadcast can drop events. It asks its author alone, queues behind every on-demand fetch on the same link, and is dropped when its manifest totals more than 1 MiB, so a misdeclared column never pulls a video unasked, and the file stays fetchable on demand, a `resolve` of it turning the running fetch on demand, asking every linked holder once its author is done.

## The archive

The peer bookkeeping lives in `main`, which the device archive does not otherwise carry, so a build with the `peer` feature carries it in both export scopes, every held entry of `_connetto_peer_entry` in its position order with its state, origin and digest, and the per-author cursors of `_connetto_peer_cursor` with their last verified offers and certificate ends. The import re-applies the provisional entries through the peer apply inside its single transaction, ahead of the restored writes, so a restored device shows its peer rows again with provenance while still offline, and the provisional records are the ones that re-apply writes. A provisional entry the importing replica's policies refuse, or one of another major, is held courier-only, and the courier-only, digest-only and retained entries are held as they are, so a restored courier still couriers. The import keeps the later of its own and the archived certificate end of each author. Per author it keeps the further of its own cursor and the archived one, skips an archived entry at or below its own cursor, and keeps its own cursor and raises `PeerChainInvalid` when the two name different heads at one sequence. The retraction log and the provisional records stay out, since the import retires the archived writer and a retired writer offers nothing. A build without the `peer` feature, the browser's among them, imports the rest and reports the entries it leaves out through `ImportPlan::peer_entries_left_out` in [archive/mod.rs](../../crates/connetto-client/src/archive/mod.rs). Peer-fetched cache chunks stay out. The one archive rule the frontier adds is that an import retires the archived writer, an enrolment or a number, and the importing replica writes under a fresh one.
